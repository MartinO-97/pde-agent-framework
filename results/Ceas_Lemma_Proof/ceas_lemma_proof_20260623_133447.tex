\section*{Cea’s Lemma for Finite Element Approximation}

Let $a: H^1_0(\Omega) \times H^1_0(\Omega) \to \mathbb{R}$ be bounded and coercive: there exist $C>0$, $c>0$ such that
\[
|a(u,v)| \le C \|u\|_1 \|v\|_1
\quad \text{and} \quad
a(v,v) \ge c \|v\|_1^2
\]
for all $u,v \in H^1_0(\Omega)$. Let $F \in H^{-1}(\Omega)$. By Lax--Milgram there exists a unique $y \in H^1_0(\Omega)$ such that
\[
a(y,v) = F(v) \quad \text{for all } v \in H^1_0(\Omega).
\]

Now choose a finite-dimensional subspace $V_h^0 \subset H^1_0(\Omega)$ and define $y_h \in V_h^0$ by
\[
a(y_h,v_h) = F(v_h) \quad \text{for all } v_h \in V_h^0.
\]
Existence and uniqueness follow from the same boundedness and coercivity restricted to $V_h^0$. Subtracting the two equations gives Galerkin orthogonality:
\[
a(y - y_h, v_h) = 0 \quad \text{for all } v_h \in V_h^0.
\]

Let $e := y - y_h$. For any $v_h \in V_h^0$ we have
\[
a(e,e) = a(y - y_h, y - y_h) = a(y - y_h, y - v_h)
\]
since $a(y - y_h, v_h) = 0$. By boundedness,
\[
|a(e,e)| \le C \|e\|_1 \|y - v_h\|_1.
\]
By coercivity,
\[
a(e,e) \ge c \|e\|_1^2.
\]
Consequently,
\[
c \|e\|_1^2 \le C \|e\|_1 \|y - v_h\|_1.
\]
If $e \neq 0$, dividing by $\|e\|_1$ yields
\[
\|e\|_1 \le \frac{C}{c}\|y - v_h\|_1
\quad \text{for all } v_h \in V_h^0.
\]
Taking the infimum over $v_h \in V_h^0$ gives
\[
\|y - y_h\|_1 \le \frac{C}{c}\inf_{v_h \in V_h^0} \|y - v_h\|_1.
\]

This is the Cea-type error estimate; the bound is independent of the mesh size $h$ and depends only on the constants $C$ and $c$. No additional regularity assumptions are required beyond Lax--Milgram and Galerkin orthogonality.
