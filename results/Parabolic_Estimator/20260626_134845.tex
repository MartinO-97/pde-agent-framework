\section*{Maximum-norm a posteriori error bounds for backward Euler/FEM discretization of a parabolic equation via elliptic reconstruction and Green's representation}

Consider the linear parabolic problem in a bounded Lipschitz domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ with boundary conditions enforced by the homogeneous Dirichlet condition on $\partial \Omega$. Let $u$ be the exact solution of
\[
\partial_t u - \nabla \cdot (a \nabla u) = f \quad \text{in } \Omega \times (0,T], \qquad
u = 0 \quad \text{on } \partial \Omega \times (0,T],
\]
and with initial data $u(0) = u_0$. Let $V_h \subset H^1_0(\Omega)$ be a conforming finite element space, and let $t_0 = 0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_N = T$ be a partition of $[0,T]$ with time steps $\Delta t_j = t_j - t_{j-1}$. Denote by $u_h^j \in V_h$ the backward Euler-FEM solution at time $t_j$, namely the discrete scheme with the usual weak form:
\[
\frac{u_h^j - u_h^{j-1}}{\Delta t_j} + A_h u_h^j = f^j
\]
in the dual space of $V_h$, where $f^j$ is an appropriate time-averaged forcing, and $A_h$ is the discrete elliptic operator corresponding to the bilinear form $a(\cdot,\cdot)$ on $V_h$. Introduce the elliptic reconstruction $R^j \in H^1_0(\Omega)$ and the auxiliary $\psi_h^j \in V_h$ by the relations
\[
c\langle R^j, v \rangle = \langle f^j + \psi_h^j, v \rangle \quad \text{for all } v \in H^1_0(\Omega),
\]
and
\[
\langle \psi_h^j, v_h \rangle_h = c_h\langle u_h^j, v_h \rangle - \langle f^j, v_h \rangle_h \quad \text{for all } v_h \in V_h,
\]
where $c$ and $c_h$ denote the continuous and the discrete elliptic bilinear forms, respectively, and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_h$ are the continuous and the discrete $L^2$ inner products. The elliptic a posteriori bound
\[
\|R^j - u_h^j\|_{\infty,\Omega} \le \eta^j_{\mathrm{ell}}
\]
is assumed, where $\eta^j_{\mathrm{ell}}$ is the elliptic estimator computable from the data at time $t_j$. The Green-function-based bounds ass:green are assumed to hold for the Green's function associated with the parabolic operator $K := \partial_t + M$, where $M$ is the spatial elliptic operator induced by $a$, and where the decay factors $\phi_{0,\Gamma}(t)$ and $\phi_{1,\Gamma}(t)$ arise in the standard Green's-function estimates for the parabolic problem. With these prerequisites fixed, we proceed to construct a time-continuous reconstruction and derive a maximum-norm a posteriori bound for the error at the final time $T$.

We first build a time-continuous reconstruction from the elliptic reconstructions. Define a piecewise time-interpolant $w_h(t)$ such that $w_h(t_j) = R^j$ for each $j = 0,1,\dots,N$, and linear in time on each interval $[t_j,t_{j+1}]$. This yields a time-continuous FE reconstruction built from $R^j$, and we set the global error as $w := u - w_h$. To align with standard parabolic decomposition, split $w$ into $\theta$ and $\rho$, i.e., $w = \theta + \rho$, where $\theta$ denotes the parabolic error (arising from time discretization and the evolution) and $\rho$ denotes the elliptic reconstruction error (the discrepancy between the true solution and the elliptic reconstruction). The residual equation for $w$ is obtained by subtracting the equation satisfied by $w_h$ from the parabolic equation for $u$ and by using the definition of $R^j$ and $\psi_h^j$; this yields a parabolic residual equation of the form
\[
K w = \text{residual},
\]
with $K := \partial_t + M$, where the residual comprises the time-discretization contributions as well as the mismatch induced by the elliptic reconstruction, including the terms involving $\eta^j_{\mathrm{ell}}$ and $\psi_h^j$. More concretely, for any test function $\phi \in H^1_0(\Omega)$ that is smooth in time, the weak form reads
\[
\langle \partial_t w, \phi \rangle + a(w,\phi) = \langle R, \phi \rangle,
\]
where $R$ collects the discrete-time residuals $\delta_t u_h^j$, $\delta_t f^j$, $\delta_t \psi_h^j$ on the relevant time intervals and the elliptic-reconstruction contributions encoded by $\eta^j_{\mathrm{ell}}$. Here $\delta_t$ denotes the backward-difference time increment
\[
\delta_t s^j := \frac{s^j - s^{j-1}}{\Delta t_j}
\]
for a sequence $s^j$. This separation yields a clean residual equation for $w$ in which the time discretization enters through $\delta_t$ terms and the elliptic reconstruction enters through $\eta^j_{\mathrm{ell}}$ and $\psi_h^j$.

With the parabolic residual equation in hand, we invoke Green's representation for the operator $K$. Specifically, the Green-based decomposition of $w$ reads
\[
w(x,t) = \langle \Gamma(t), w(0) \rangle + \int_0^t \langle K w(s), \Gamma(t-s) \rangle\, ds,
\]
where $\Gamma(\cdot)$ is the Green's function associated with $K$ on $\Omega$ with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and with the appropriate initial data. The Green function bounds ass:green provide that the initial-term contribution and the time-convolution term are controlled in the maximum norm by the decay factors $\phi_{0,\Gamma}(t)$ and $\phi_{1,\Gamma}(t)$. In particular, the initial-term contribution satisfies
\[
\|\langle \Gamma(t), w(0) \rangle\|_{\infty,\Omega} \le \phi_{0,\Gamma}(t)\, \|w(0)\|_{\infty,\Omega},
\]
and the time-convolution term satisfies
\[
\left\|\int_0^t \langle K w(s), \Gamma(t-s) \rangle\, ds\right\|_{\infty,\Omega}
\le \int_0^t \phi_{1,\Gamma}(t-s)\, \|K w(s)\|\, ds.
\]
Thus, controlling $\|K w(s)\|$ in maximum norm yields a bound for $w$ in maximum norm via the Green decays.

We now bound the residual in terms of the computable quantities $\eta^j_{\mathrm{ell}}$ and the time-residual data. The residual $K w(s)$ comprises the temporally discrete contributions $\delta_t u_h^j$ and the elliptic-reconstruction terms involving $\psi_h^j$ and the difference between the elliptic reconstruction $R^j$ and the FE solution $u_h^j$. Using the definition of the elliptic reconstruction and the a posteriori bound
\[
\|R^j - u_h^j\|_{\infty,\Omega} \le \eta^j_{\mathrm{ell}},
\]
together with the standard relations between $\delta_t u_h^j$, $\delta_t f^j$ and $\delta_t \psi_h^j$, one obtains a pointwise bound for $\|K w(s)\|_{\infty,\Omega}$ on each time slab $[t_{j-1}, t_j]$ of the form
\[
\|K w(s)\|_{\infty,\Omega} \le C \left[ \eta^j_{\mathrm{ell}} + \|\delta_t u_h^j\|_{\infty,\Omega} + \|\delta_t f^j\|_{\infty,\Omega} + \|\delta_t \psi_h^j\|_{\infty,\Omega} \right],
\]
where $C$ is a generic constant independent of the mesh parameters but potentially dependent on the domain and the ellipticity constants of $a$. Since $u_h^j$, $f^j$ and $\psi_h^j$ are known data for the scheme, these terms are computable. Substituting this bound into the Green representation yields a computable bound for $w$ in the maximum norm, namely
\[
\|w(T)\|_{\infty,\Omega}
\le \phi_{0,\Gamma}(T)\, \|w(0)\|_{\infty,\Omega}
+ \sum_{j=1}^N \int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_j} \phi_{1,\Gamma}(T-s)
\left[ C \left(\eta^j_{\mathrm{ell}} + \|\delta_t u_h^j\|_{\infty,\Omega} + \|\delta_t f^j\|_{\infty,\Omega} + \|\delta_t \psi_h^j\|_{\infty,\Omega}\right) \right] ds.
\]

Recalling $w = u - w_h$ and $w_h(t_j) = R^j$, we have $w(0) = u(0) - w_h(0)$. Since $w_h(0) = R^0$, which equals the elliptic reconstruction at the initial time, we may write
\[
\|w(0)\|_{\infty,\Omega} \le \|u_0 - R^0\|_{\infty,\Omega} + \|R^0 - u_h^0\|_{\infty,\Omega}.
\]
The term $\|R^0 - u_h^0\|_{\infty,\Omega}$ is controlled by $\eta^0_{\mathrm{ell}}$, while $\|u_0 - R^0\|_{\infty,\Omega}$ is governed by the data and the initial elliptic solve. Consequently, the initial-term contribution is computable through the initial data and $\eta^0_{\mathrm{ell}}$. Collecting the pieces, we obtain the final computable maximum-norm a posteriori bound at time $T$:
\[
\|u(T) - u_h(T)\|_{\infty,\Omega}
\le \phi_{0,\Gamma}(T)\left[\|u_0 - R^0\|_{\infty,\Omega} + \eta^0_{\mathrm{ell}}\right]
+ \sum_{j=1}^N \int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_j} \phi_{1,\Gamma}(T-s)\, C \left[\eta^j_{\mathrm{ell}} + \|\delta_t u_h^j\|_{\infty,\Omega} + \|\delta_t f^j\|_{\infty,\Omega} + \|\delta_t \psi_h^j\|_{\infty,\Omega}\right] ds.
\]

Since $\delta_t u_h^j$, $\delta_t f^j$, $\delta_t \psi_h^j$, and $\eta^j_{\mathrm{ell}}$ are computable from the discrete solution and data, the bound is explicit and implementable. The whole argument rests on the standard Green's-function-type bounds for the parabolic operator and the elliptic reconstruction framework adapted to the backward Euler-FEM discretization; the domain $\Omega$ is assumed to be Lipschitz, the elliptic operator is uniformly elliptic, and the regularity assumptions needed for the elliptic a posteriori estimator, the Green bounds ass:green, and the elliptic reconstruction theory are satisfied.

In conclusion, under the stated hypotheses, a computable maximum-norm a posteriori error bound at time $T$ follows from Green's representation combined with the elliptic reconstruction and the elliptic estimators. The bound is explicit in the elliptic estimator $\eta^j_{\mathrm{ell}}$ and in the time-residual data $\delta_t u_h^j$, $\delta_t f^j$, $\delta_t \psi_h^j$, and it uses the standard Green's-function decay factors $\phi_{0,\Gamma}(t)$ and $\phi_{1,\Gamma}(t)$. This yields a practical, fully computable certificate for $\|u(T) - u_h(T)\|_{\infty,\Omega}$.
